Sausage, pancetta and white bean braise with sage, charred sourdough

Four proper pork sausages skinned and crumbled through a pan of onion, celery, carrot, courgette, tomatoes and cannellini beans, pancetta rendered first for the fat, green beans and cavolo nero in at the end, with sage, Dijon and a sharp edge of cider vinegar; griddled garlic sourdough for scooping.

Start to plate Hob one wide pan with lid, plus griddle pan

      Method

        Notes

        Render the pancetta slowly in a dry pan before anything else; its fat browns the sausages. Half the crisp bits are held back for the top.

        No salt in the base: pancetta, sausage and stock carry plenty. Correct at the end, after tasting.

        Skin the sausages and brown the meat in rough nuggets rather than slicing: more crust, and the seasoning spreads through the whole pan.

        Toast the fennel seeds in the pork fat for half a minute before the veg goes in - that is where the aniseed note comes from.

        Tomato purée goes in with the Dijon and cooks a minute until it darkens a shade, then the vinegar; cooking it out is what turns it savoury.

        Green beans go in three minutes before the cavolo nero so both finish just tender; neither should be simmered from the start.

        Swap for what the box has: hispi for the cavolo nero, tenderstem for the green beans, a tin of chopped tomatoes if the fresh ones are gone.

        Four Packington Olde English sausages is about 365 g, plus 70 g pancetta as seasoning - roughly 220 g of pork a head against a tin of beans and the best part of a kilo of veg. Celery and toasted fennel seed stand in for a fennel bulb; stock plus cider vinegar stands in for cider. The last two sausages: fry gently for breakfast within two days, or freeze on the day.Your Kitchen Brain kit 2.6.0
